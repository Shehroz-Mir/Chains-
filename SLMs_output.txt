Small Language Models (SLMs): Overview, Model Guide, When to Use, and Q&A

Overview
- SLMs are small, fast, and efficient language models that can run on edge devices like phones, tablets, and smartwatches. They excel at low-latency, real-time use.
- They are generally weaker than large LLMs at complex reasoning and broad knowledge tasks, but they offer practical trade-offs for cost, speed, and deployment flexibility.

When to Use SLMs
- Ideal for edge-device deployment, low-latency interactions, and limited compute or memory budgets.
- Choose larger SLMs when tasks require better generation, reasoning, or longer context; choose smaller SLMs for speed, battery efficiency, and strict on-device constraints.

Model Guide (selected SLMs)
- Qwen2 (0.5B / 1B / 7B): Scalable open-source family. 0.5B for ultra-light apps; 7B for stronger generation and summarization.
- Mistral Nemo 12B: Open source, strong for translation and dialogue; good balance of capability and local deployment.
- Llama 3.1 8B: Solid power/efficiency mix; good for QA and sentiment; note some usage restrictions.
- Pythia (160M–2.8B): Transparent and customizable; excels at coding and reasoning; weaker on broad language tasks.
- Cerebras-GPT (111M–2.7B): Very compute-efficient (Chinchilla scaling); a good choice when resources are tight.
- Phi-3.5 (3.8B): 128K context, multilingual; strong on long documents, multi-turn chats, and reasoning; research license.
- StableLM-Zephyr (3B): Fast; good at reasoning and role-play; less suited to complex writing and coding.
- TinyLlama (1.1B): Efficient, edge-friendly; strong commonsense for its size.
- MobileLLaMA (1.4B): Optimized for mobile/low-latency; faster than many peers at similar size.
- LaMini-GPT (774M–1.5B): Multilingual, distilled for instruction following; best for focused tasks rather than deep context.

5 Questions and Answers on Small Language Models

1) What is the main advantage of Small Language Models (SLMs) compared to Large Language Models (LLMs)?
- SLMs are compact, efficient, and don’t need massive servers. They’re built for speed and real-time performance and can run on smartphones, tablets, or smartwatches, unlike LLMs which require significant computational resources.

2) What makes Phi-3.5 unique among the small language models mentioned?
- Phi-3.5 has 128K tokens of context length, enabling it to handle long documents or multi-turn conversations without losing context. It’s multilingual and can compete with larger models like Llama 13B and GPT-3.5 while having much lower computational demands.

3) Which small language model is specifically optimized for mobile and low-power devices?
- MobileLLaMA is specifically designed for mobile and low-power devices. With 1.4 billion parameters, it’s optimized for speed and low-latency applications, performing about 40% faster than comparable models while maintaining strong performance.

4) What is Pythia particularly good at compared to other small language models?
- Pythia (160M–2.8B) is designed for reasoning and coding tasks. It excels at structured, logic-based work and outperforms some peers (e.g., GPT-Neo) in coding environments where logical thinking is required.

5) How was LaMini-GPT developed, and what is it optimized for?
- LaMini-GPT was developed via knowledge distillation from larger GPT-family models and trained on over 2.58 million instruction–response pairs. It’s optimized for multilingual tasks and instruction-following in resource-constrained environments, with sizes from 774M to 1.5B parameters.
