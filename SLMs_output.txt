Small Language Models (SLMs): Overview, Model Highlights, Best Picks, and FAQ

Overview
- SLMs are small, efficient models built for speed and real-time use on phones, tablets, and wearables. They trade some deep reasoning for low latency and low resource use compared to large language models (LLMs).

Model highlights
- Qwen2 (0.5B/1B/7B)
  - 0.5B for ultra-light apps; 7B for stronger summarization and text generation.
  - Fast, resource‑friendly; weaker at complex reasoning than big LLMs.
- Mistral Nemo 12B
  - Strong for translation and dialogue; competes with larger models while still runnable locally.
- Llama 3.1 8B
  - Good balance of power and efficiency; solid for QA and sentiment; fast without big compute.
- Pythia (160M–2.8B)
  - Built for reasoning and coding; customizable and transparent.
  - Weaker on broad language versus Phi‑3.5 and Llama 3.1 8B.
- Cerebras‑GPT (111M–2.7B)
  - Very compute‑efficient (Chinchilla scaling); good when resources are tight.
  - Less broadly trained than big models.
- Phi‑3.5 (3.8B)
  - 128K context and multilingual; great for long docs, multi‑turn chats, and reasoning with low compute.
- StableLM‑Zephyr (3B)
  - Fast on edge devices; good at reasoning and role‑play; less suited to long‑form writing/coding than larger models.
- TinyLlama (1.1B)
  - Strong efficiency for mobile/edge; better than Pythia‑1.4B on commonsense; not as strong as 13B models.
- MobileLLaMA (1.4B; also 2.7B)
  - Optimized for on‑device, low‑latency AI; about 40% faster than similar small models; near 3B‑class performance.
- LaMini‑GPT (774M–1.5B)
  - Multilingual, distilled for instruction following; excels on focused tasks; weaker on broad, deep context.

Licensing and availability
- Most models are open source, though some have usage restrictions (for example, Llama usage terms; Phi is research‑only).

Best picks by need
- Ultra‑light deployment: Qwen2‑0.5B, TinyLlama
- Coding and reasoning focus: Pythia
- Long‑context and multilingual: Phi‑3.5
- Maximum on‑device speed: MobileLLaMA, StableLM‑Zephyr
- Balanced general use: Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen2‑7B

FAQ: 5 questions and answers about SLMs

1) What is the main advantage of Small Language Models (SLMs) compared to Large Language Models (LLMs)?
- SLMs are compact, efficient, and don’t require massive servers. They’re built for speed and real-time performance and can run directly on personal devices like smartphones, tablets, or smartwatches, unlike LLMs which need significant computational infrastructure.

2) What makes Phi‑3.5 unique among the small language models discussed?
- Phi‑3.5 stands out with its 128K tokens of context length, which allows it to handle long documents or multi‑turn conversations without losing context. It’s also multilingual and can compete with larger models like Llama 13B and GPT‑3.5 while having much lower computational demands.

3) Which model is specifically optimized for mobile and low‑power devices?
- MobileLLaMA is specifically designed for mobile and low‑power devices. With 1.4 billion parameters, it’s optimized for speed and low‑latency AI applications, performing about 40% faster than comparable models while maintaining strong performance.

4) What is Pythia particularly good at compared to other small language models?
- Pythia (ranging from 160 million to 2.8 billion parameters) is specifically designed for reasoning and coding tasks. It excels at handling structured, logic‑based tasks and performs better than models like GPT‑Neo in coding environments, though it may be less consistent in broader general language tasks.

5) How was LaMini‑GPT developed and what is it optimized for?
- LaMini‑GPT was developed through knowledge distillation from larger GPT family models and trained on over 2.58 million instruction‑response pairs. It’s specifically optimized for multilingual tasks and instruction‑following in resource‑constrained environments, though it may not be ideal for tasks requiring deep contextual understanding.
